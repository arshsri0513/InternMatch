# Build Log — Entry 5: PMF Case

## PMF hypothesis
**User:** College students actively searching for internships.

**Situation:** They have several potential opportunities and need to judge which ones fit their skills and preferences.

**Outcome:** They want to narrow options and make a more informed choice with less repeated checking.

**Alternative:** They currently use job portals, professional networks, college groups, and personal contacts, then manually compare information.

**Edge:** A focused comparison flow could put the decision-relevant details side by side instead of requiring repeated switching between listings.

**Falsifiability:** This hypothesis could be disproved if target students do not experience comparison friction, already have an equally effective workflow, or do not find side-by-side comparison useful.

## ICP
**Working ICP:** College students in India who are actively searching for internships and currently use at least one online platform plus a college/personal channel.

## Current substitute
Likely substitutes include job portals, professional networks, college WhatsApp/Telegram groups, spreadsheets/notes, and asking seniors or peers. The actual dominant substitute for the ICP is still an open research question.

## Substitute cost
Potential costs include time spent switching between sources, repeated checking of eligibility/deadlines, and difficulty comparing alternatives. No numeric cost is claimed yet because it requires field evidence.

### Past-behaviour interview script
- Tell me about the last time you searched for an internship.
- Where did you look first?
- What did you do after finding an opportunity?
- What information did you compare before deciding?
- Did you have to switch between multiple sources?
- Tell me about the last time you rejected an opportunity. Why?
- What was frustrating or time-consuming?
- What did you do instead?

## Mechanism
The prototype creates value by collecting decision-relevant opportunity details into a compact comparison view. The mechanism being tested is **less switching and easier side-by-side evaluation**, not "AI" as a feature.

## Opportunity sizing
**Status: deliberately not monetised yet.**  
The current prototype is a student-facing utility, and a defensible paid market estimate requires evidence about who would pay, what the payer is, and what price is realistic. No unsupported TAM/SAM/SOM figure is included.
